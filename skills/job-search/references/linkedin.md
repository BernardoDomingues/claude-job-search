# LinkedIn profile

A profile works in two situations: a recruiter **opens** it after an application, and a
recruiter **searches** for people with a skill set and the profile shows up (or not).
Optimize for both. Ask the user to paste their current profile text (or an export) —
never guess its content.

## Section by section

| Section | Aim |
|---|---|
| Photo & banner | clear, recent, professional face photo; a simple banner (stack, role or a neutral image) |
| Headline | role + core stack + what you deliver, in the words recruiters search for — e.g. "Backend Engineer · Node.js, TypeScript, PostgreSQL · payments & integrations" |
| About | 3–5 short paragraphs: who you are professionally, main strengths with evidence, what you are looking for. First two lines must work alone (they show before "see more") |
| Experience | same outcome-focused bullets as the résumé (see [resume-and-cover-letter.md](resume-and-cover-letter.md)); company, title and dates consistent with the résumé |
| Skills | the stack you want to be found for, ordered by relevance; pin the top ones |
| Featured | projects, talks, articles, open-source work |
| Recommendations | from managers or peers who can speak to results |
| Location & languages | set where you really are and the languages you work in |

## Being found

- Recruiters search by keywords: put the exact terms (frameworks, languages, role names)
  in the headline, about, experience and skills — where they are true.
- Use the "Open to work" setting with specific job titles and locations; choose whether
  it is visible to recruiters only or to everyone.
- Write in the language of the market you target; LinkedIn supports a secondary-language
  profile if the user targets two markets.
- A complete, active profile (recent posts or comments in the field) tends to surface
  more; quality over volume.

## Review output

Give the user a proposed text for each section they want to change, explain why, and keep
everything consistent with `profile/resume.md`. Do not invent achievements to fill a
section — leave it short instead.

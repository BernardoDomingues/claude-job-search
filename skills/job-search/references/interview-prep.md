# Interview preparation (behavioral and fit)

Scope: recruiter screens, behavioral rounds and hiring-manager conversations. Technical
interviews (live coding, system design) are out of scope for this plugin — point the user
to dedicated material if they ask.

## Pre-interview kit (fill one per interview, in `generated/<job-id>/interview-prep.md`)

1. **The role** — must-haves from the posting, and the user's best evidence for each.
2. **The company** — what it does, product, customers, size/stage, recent news; why the
   user wants it (in their words).
3. **The format** — who is interviewing, how long, which stage. Ask the recruiter if
   unknown.
4. **Story bank** — 5–7 STAR stories mapped to likely themes (below).
5. **Pitch** — a 60–90 second "tell me about yourself".
6. **Gaps** — requirements the user lacks, and an honest way to address each.
7. **Questions to ask** — 3–5, specific to this company.
8. **Logistics** — time zone, link, camera/mic test, salary expectation ready (see
   [negotiation.md](negotiation.md)).

## STAR answers

Behavioral questions ("Tell me about a time…") are best answered as a short story:

- **S**ituation — the context, one or two sentences
- **T**ask — what you were responsible for
- **A**ction — what *you* did (say "I", not "we"); most of the answer goes here
- **R**esult — the outcome, with a number if possible, and what you learned

Aim for 1–2 minutes per answer. Build stories only from the user's real experience —
help them choose, structure and trim, never invent details. One good story can cover
several themes.

## Common themes

- A project you are proud of / biggest impact
- A conflict or disagreement with a colleague or manager
- A mistake or failure and what changed afterwards
- Working under a tight deadline or with unclear requirements
- Taking ownership beyond your role / leading without authority
- Learning something new quickly
- Receiving hard feedback
- Why this company, why are you leaving, where you want to be in a few years

## Practice mode

When the user wants to rehearse: ask one question at a time, wait for their answer,
then give short feedback (structure, specificity, length, result clarity) and a tighter
version in their own voice. Offer a follow-up question like a real interviewer would.
If the interview is in a second language, keep sentences short and words common, and
point out phrases that may sound unnatural.

## Questions the candidate can ask

- What would success look like in the first 90 days?
- What is the team working on now, and what is the biggest challenge?
- How are decisions made about technical direction / priorities?
- How do you support growth — feedback, promotion, learning?
- What are the next steps in the process and the timeline?

# Evaluating and negotiating an offer

Negotiating is expected in most hiring processes; asking politely, with reasons, rarely
costs an offer. What damages a negotiation is an ultimatum, a bluff that gets called, or
a tone that makes the employer doubt the person wants the job.

## Before talking numbers

1. **Know your walk-away point** — the minimum total package the user would accept, and
   their best alternative if this offer fails (another offer, current job, freelancing).
   Negotiation literature calls this the BATNA (best alternative to a negotiated
   agreement). A strong alternative is the main source of leverage.
2. **Research the range** — salary surveys and public ranges for the role, seniority and
   location; similar postings that disclose pay; people in the user's network.
3. **Compare like with like** — convert every offer to the same period and currency, and
   account for contract type (employee vs. contractor: taxes, benefits, paid leave,
   equipment, notice period, currency risk). A contractor rate usually needs to be well
   above an equivalent salary to break even.

## When asked for expectations early

Options, from safest to most direct:
- Ask for the budgeted range first ("What range is budgeted for this role?").
- Give a researched range whose bottom is still acceptable, tied to the role's scope.
- If a number must be given, base it on the research, not on current pay.

## Responding to an offer

1. Thank them and show enthusiasm for the role.
2. Ask for the offer in writing and time to review (a few days is normal).
3. Clarify everything: base, bonus, equity, benefits, start date, contract type,
   working hours, location rules, review cycle.
4. Make **one** consolidated counter, not a series of small asks.
5. Justify it with value and market data (or a competing offer, only if real).
6. If base is fixed, ask about other levers: signing bonus, earlier review, title,
   equipment/education budget, extra leave, start date, remote flexibility.
7. Get the final agreement in writing.

## Template (adapt the brackets, keep it short)

> Thank you for the offer — I'm excited about [team/product] and about joining.
> Based on [the scope of the role / market data for this level / another offer I'm
> considering], I was expecting a base of [amount]. If you can get to [amount], I'm ready
> to accept and start on [date]. Is there flexibility there?

## Evaluating an offer checklist

- Total compensation per year, in the user's currency, after taxes where relevant
- Stability: company stage, funding, layoffs history, contract length
- Growth: scope, mentors, promotion path, learning budget
- Fit: team, manager, working hours across time zones, on-call
- Hidden costs: equipment, accounting (contractors), commute, currency fees
